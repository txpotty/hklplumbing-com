    Automatic Grease Removal Units in Aiea

    An automatic grease removal unit heats the trapped grease and skims it into a container on a timer, so staff empty a bucket instead of scraping a tank. They cost more up front. Plan for a dedicated circuit near the unit, which your electrician will need to run. Fry-heavy kitchens like them most.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Aiea, HI

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Without it, a dumped sink pushes water through faster than the unit is rated for and the grease goes out the outlet. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Aiea

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. The air intake has to be vented to code, not left open in the kitchen. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. A trap that sits out of level loses capacity, so we shim it flat before connecting anything.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Installation in Aiea

    Outdoor gravity interceptors run from 750 gallons to well past 2,000, and setting one means an excavator on site and a traffic-rated lid if it sits in the drive lane. We confirm the H-20 load rating before the tank ships. Most go in over two to four days. The inlet invert has to sit a couple of inches above the outlet, and we shoot both with a laser level before backfill.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Aiea

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. The drawings and the equipment list from your kitchen designer tell us what will drain where. Changes get expensive. Saw-cutting a finished floor to relocate a trap can add several days to a build-out, so we flag sizing or location problems at plan review.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Aiea

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. You get a written, itemized number after we see the kitchen.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow drains near the trap and failed inspections point to replacement too.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. Strictly, a grease trap is the small indoor unit rated in gallons per minute, and an interceptor is the large tank rated in gallons of capacity. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. We like the plastic bodies from Schier where steel would rust out. Brand matters less than sizing. We pick the rating first, then the brand that fits the space and budget.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. For fry-heavy kitchens we also set automatic units that skim grease on a timer. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    On a commercial kitchen, no. The work needs a licensed plumber and a permit in nearly every jurisdiction. A homeowner adding a small under-sink trap to a home kitchen may be allowed to do it, but even then the flow control and vent have to be right or the trap siphons and does nothing. Ask the building department first.

    An under-sink hydromechanical trap with good access is usually a half-day job, including the flow control and a leak test. Floor-recessed units take longer because we cut and patch concrete. Outdoor tanks are different. A buried interceptor runs two to five working days after permit, mostly driven by the dig and the paving repair.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. We file both when needed and give you copies of the approvals, which your health inspector may ask to see at opening. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.
