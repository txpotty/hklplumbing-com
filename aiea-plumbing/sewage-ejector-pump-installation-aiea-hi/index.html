    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Aiea

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Aiea

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so the toilet, shower and sink drain to a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We saw the floor, trench drains to the basin, set it, carry a two-inch vent to the roof and run discharge to the main drain through a check valve and shutoff. Then we patch the concrete.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Aiea

    A high water alarm should be on any ejector basin, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. Tropical downpours can drop heavy rain in a short time, and flash flooding in valleys can overwhelm drains and push debris into sewer lines. Battery backup is an option.
