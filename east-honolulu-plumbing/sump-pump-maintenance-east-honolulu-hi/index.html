    Maintenance Versus Replacement Cost in East Honolulu, HI

    Battery Backup System Testing in East Honolulu, HI

    Storms knock out power when the pit fills fastest. A backup pump exists for exactly that hour. Our plumber reads the battery date, cleans corrosion off the terminals and tests the charger, because a failed charger can leave the battery flat within a season.

    Annual Sump Pump Check-Ups in East Honolulu

    Once a year is the minimum. Each yearly visit covers the motor, the impeller, the float and the discharge line. Then we fill the pit and time how long the pump takes to empty it, because a slow drawdown is the earliest sign of a worn impeller or a partly blocked line.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in East Honolulu, HI

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. We check the battery with a load tester, not just a voltmeter. Then we unplug the main pump and let the backup empty the pit on its own. Alarms get tested too.

    Commercial Sump Pump Maintenance in East Honolulu, HI

    Commercial pits are often bigger and deeper, and many have two pumps on an alternating controller. We test both pumps, the alternator and the high-water alarm. You get a written record of each reading, which comes in handy for insurance renewals and inspections. Quarterly visits are common.

    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in East Honolulu, HI

    Float trouble causes more no-start calls than anything else. Tethered floats can wrap around the discharge pipe or snag on the pit wall. Vertical floats hang up once minerals coat the rod. For narrow basins where a float keeps catching, an electronic switch with no moving float is a good swap that ends the problem.

    Check Valve and Discharge Pipe Repair in East Honolulu, HI

    A bad check valve can nearly double how often the pump starts. Each start heats the motor windings. Our plumbers replace worn valves with quieter spring-loaded types and check the pipe for cracks and loose couplings out to where it leaves the house. Discharge needs to land far enough from the slab or footings that it does not seep right back into the pit.
