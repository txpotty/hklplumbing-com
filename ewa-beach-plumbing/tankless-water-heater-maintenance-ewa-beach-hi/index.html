    Tankless Error Code Diagnosis in Ewa Beach, HI

    An error code is the heater telling you what it saw. On a Rinnai an LC code means scale, on a Navien E003 means no ignition, and on a Noritz a 90 usually means an air or exhaust problem. We pull the code history before touching anything. If a part has failed, you hear the repair and the price before we start.

    Navien Condensing Tune-Ups in Ewa Beach

    Navien NPE units are condensing heaters with a second heat exchanger that pulls extra heat out of the exhaust before it leaves the house. That second exchanger makes acidic condensate. We clear the condensate trap and check the neutralizer media. On A-series models with the built-in recirculation pump, we test the pump and the buffer tank too.

    What Tankless Maintenance Saves in Ewa Beach

    Service Valve Kit Installs in Ewa Beach, HI

    Plenty of tankless heaters went in without service valves, and without them there is no clean way to hook up a descaling pump. We add a pair of isolation valves with purge ports on the hot and cold lines. Most kits include a relief valve port as well. Future flushes then take minutes to set up.

    Yearly Tankless Inspection in Ewa Beach

    A yearly inspection costs less than one service call for a dead heater. We soap-test the gas connections and try the pressure relief valve. We pull the air filter and check the igniter. Anything that is wearing out goes on a written list with a plain price, and you decide what gets done now and what can wait until next year.

    Multi-Unit Tankless Checks in Ewa Beach, HI

    Restaurants and salons often run two or more tankless heaters on a shared manifold, and one weak unit can drag the whole bank down. Gas pressure gets read with every unit firing together. Each heater gets its own flow and temperature reading. Then we compare them.

    Step-by-Step Tankless Flushing in Ewa Beach

    First we shut off the gas and power, then close both isolation valves so the heater is cut off from the house plumbing. The pump pulls solution from a bucket, through the cold port, out the hot port and back again. Once the cycle ends, we rinse with clean water and pull the inlet screen. Then we check for leaks.
