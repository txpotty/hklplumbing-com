    Well-X-Trol Pressure Tank Installation in Hawaiian Paradise Park, HI

    Amtrol's Well-X-Trol line uses a diaphragm design. We put in plenty of them. Its stainless or polypropylene water connection keeps water off the steel shell, which helps the tank resist rust from the inside, and the factory pre-charge still gets checked and adjusted to your switch on install.

    Bladder-Style Pressure Tank Replacement in Hawaiian Paradise Park, HI

    A bladder tank holds water inside a rubber balloon surrounded by compressed air. The air pushes water out when you open a tap. Bladders crack with age, especially if the tank ran with low pre-charge, which waterlogs the tank. Replacement is the fix.

    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Hawaiian Paradise Park

    Most homes on a well have a tank between 20 and 86 gallons sitting near the pressure switch. First step, kill the breaker to the pump. Then we drain the system, unthread the old tank from the tank tee, set the pre-charge on the new one, and connect it with new fittings and fresh sealant. Usually a morning.

    Constant Pressure System Conversion in Hawaiian Paradise Park, HI

    Constant pressure systems use a variable speed drive that adjusts pump speed to demand. You get even pressure across fixtures. These systems can run on a smaller tank, though the drive and wiring add to the price. We explain the trade-off.

    Flex-Lite Pressure Tank Replacement in Hawaiian Paradise Park

    Flex-Lite tanks have a composite shell. Rust is not a concern. That makes them a good pick for damp basements, crawlspaces and well houses where steel tanks tend to corrode from the bottom up, and they are lighter to carry into tight spaces. Same plumbing as steel.

    Galvanized Steel Pressure Tank Upgrades in Hawaiian Paradise Park

    Old galvanized tanks have no bladder at all. Water sits right against an air pocket. Over time the water absorbs the air, the tank waterlogs, which means draining it to restore the air. A sealed bladder tank ends that chore.

    Pressure Tanks for Well Houses and Barns in Hawaiian Paradise Park, HI

    Tanks in outbuildings face heat, cold and damp. Moisture eats steel tanks from the bottom. We often recommend a composite tank in those spaces, raise it off the floor on a stand, and insulate exposed piping and the switch when the building sees freezing weather. Simple protection.

    Pressure Switch and Gauge Integration in Hawaiian Paradise Park, HI

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Switch and tank are a team. A switch set at 40/60 needs a tank pre-charged to 38 psi, and a gauge that reads correctly so you can see what the system is doing, so we replace a worn gauge rather than trusting a sticky needle.
