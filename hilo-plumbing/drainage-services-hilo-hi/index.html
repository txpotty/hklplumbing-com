    French Drain Installation in Hilo

    A French drain is a gravel-filled trench with perforated pipe that collects water and carries it away from problem areas. It works well along foundations, behind retaining walls and in low spots that hold water after storms. We wrap the gravel in filter fabric so fine soil does not clog the system. The drain is sloped to an outlet, dry well or permitted discharge point.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Hilo

    Drain clearing in Hilo typically runs $125 to $500 depending on the line, and hydro jetting costs $400 to $900. Yard drainage projects such as French drains or dry wells typically range from $1,500 to $6,000. We give you a firm price after inspecting the problem.

    We commonly install and service quality sump pump brands such as Zoeller, Little Giant, Wayne, and Liberty Pumps. Our technicians can help you choose the best model for your specific needs, considering factors like horsepower, pump type, and reliability for Hawaii weather conditions.

    Drain snaking uses a flexible auger to physically break through or retrieve a blockage. Hydro-jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the inside of pipes, removing buildup like grease, scale, and roots, providing a more thorough cleaning. Hydro-jetting is usually more effective for severe or recurring clogs.

    While readily available, chemical drain cleaners are generally not recommended. They often contain harsh chemicals that can corrode pipes over time, especially older ones, and can be dangerous if not handled properly. Professional plumbers use gentler, more effective methods.

    Tree roots are instinctively attracted to the moisture and nutrients found inside sewer lines. Even tiny cracks or loose joints in older pipes can allow roots to penetrate, growing larger over time and causing severe blockages and pipe damage. This is a common issue in plenty of established Hilo, HI neighborhoods with mature trees.

    A standard residential drain cleaning for a single fixture typically takes about one to two hours. More complex jobs, like main sewer line clearing or hydro-jetting, can take longer, potentially three to four hours or more depending on the severity of the blockage.

    Common signs include slow drains, gurgling sounds from pipes or toilets, unpleasant odors coming from drains, water backing up into sinks or showers, and multiple fixtures draining slowly at once. If you notice any of these, it's time to call a plumber.

    Simple downspout extensions are a fair DIY project. French drains and dry wells require correct slope, fabric and outlet placement, and mistakes can push water toward the house. Professional installation also handles utility locating and any permits.
