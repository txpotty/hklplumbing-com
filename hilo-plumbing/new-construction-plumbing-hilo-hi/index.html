    Some jurisdictions allow owner-builders to do their own plumbing on a home they will live in, but the work still has to pass every inspection. Under-slab and gas work are the hardest to get right and the most expensive to fix later. Many owner-builders hire us for those phases and finish simpler tasks themselves.

    We usually use PEX-A for water supply because it handles heat and resists the scale and pinhole problems copper can develop with aggressive or hard water. Drains are usually PVC or ABS, depending on local code and builder preference. Copper is an option for mains or exposed runs when you want it.

    Homes near the ocean often see green corrosion on copper lines and hose bibs, and stainless or plastic fittings tend to hold up better there. Frozen pipes are essentially a non-issue for homes in Hawaii, since sea-level and most residential areas never get cold enough to freeze water lines. We select materials and installation methods that can handle local conditions, insulating pipes and burying lines at adequate depths where freezing is a risk. We also use materials that can handle thermal expansion and contraction, preventing leaks and bursts.

    For new construction plumbing, we primarily use PEX piping for water supply lines due to its flexibility, durability, and resistance to scale build-up. For drain, waste, and vent systems, we use PVC pipes, which are corrosion-resistant and long-lasting.

Most municipal water in Hawaii comes from volcanic groundwater aquifers and is generally soft to moderate, so heavy scale is less of a problem than in many places. A softener loop is easy to add during the rough-in phase to protect your entire plumbing system and appliances from mineral buildup.
