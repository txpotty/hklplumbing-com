    Kitchen drains start failing at the basket strainer. Old strainer putty dries out and the locknut rusts, so water runs down the tailpiece and looks like a trap leak. We replace the strainer and trap together. Disposal sinks get a new flange instead.

    Public restroom drains take constant use.     Kitchen Drain and Disposal Tie-In in Hilo

    Disposals change the kitchen drain. The disposal outlet must sit above the trap, the dishwasher hose needs a high loop or air gap, and nothing can pull sideways. They all get set together. Then the disposal runs with water while we watch.

    Choosing Drain Material in Hilo, HI

    PVC is the default choice under most sinks. It will not rust and costs less, while thick brass is worth it where the trap shows, like under a pedestal. ABS works the same way. We match what you have, with a proper transition where they meet.

    Leaking P-Trap Replacement in Hilo

    Traps leak at the joints first. The beveled washers inside the slip nuts flatten and harden over the years, and a trap that has been taken apart several times to clear clogs often has stripped plastic threads or a cracked nut. Trap kits are cheap. Labor is most of the bill.

    Pop-Up Drain Assembly Replacement in Hilo, HI

    A stopper that drops on its own has a worn pivot. The plastic ball on the pivot rod wears flat, the nut that holds it loses its seal, and the rod no longer holds the stopper up, so the sink either will not drain or will not hold water. One new pop-up fixes every part of that. Takes about an hour.

    Utility Sink Drain Replacement in Hilo

    Laundry tubs see lint and paint water. Over time that wears the thin plastic drain and trap on a utility sink until it cracks, and lint from the washer standpipe often builds up in the trap so badly that it never drains at full speed again. A 1-1/2 inch trap with a cleanout plug goes in. A lint trap on the washer hose helps.
