    Control Valve and Timer Diagnostics in Hilo, HI

    The control valve runs every stage of regeneration, from backwash through brine draw to the final refill. We advance it manually through each cycle and watch for a motor that stalls, a drive gear that skips, a piston that drags or a drain that never shuts off. Worn pistons turn up a lot.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Hilo, HI

    Plans take the remembering off your plate. You get a reminder call before each yearly visit, and if the control valve fails between visits you get priority booking. Repairs found during a plan visit are billed at a reduced rate.

    Commercial Twin-Tank Maintenance in Hilo

    Salons, car washes, hotels and restaurants put far more gallons through a softener than any house does, and a hard-water day can mean spotted glassware or a scaled ice machine. On twin-tank and progressive-flow units, we spend extra time on the meter and the tank switching. Visits run monthly or quarterly, depending on volume.

    Hardness Testing at the Tap in Hilo

    We test with a titration kit, counting drops until the sample changes color, because test strips are too rough for setting a valve. We take the raw sample from an unsoftened hose bib or with the softener on bypass. Softened water comes from the kitchen tap.

    Regeneration Programming and Hardness Testing in Hilo, HI

    Plenty of softeners still run factory settings. Either the installer left the defaults in, or the family got bigger and the settings stayed put. We enter your raw hardness and household size, then set a metered valve to regenerate by gallons used instead of every few days on a timer.

    Resin Rebedding in Hilo

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water slowly swell and soften resin beads, and iron or sediment can coat them until capacity drops off. We try a resin cleaner first. If the bed is spent, we drain the tank, pull the old resin and refill it with fresh high-capacity resin, which runs far less than a new softener.

    Yearly Water Softener Tune-Ups in Hilo

    A tune-up starts with two hardness readings and ends with a full manual regeneration that we watch from start to finish. In between, we check the salt level, the brine well and the float. Figure on an hour for a typical unit.
