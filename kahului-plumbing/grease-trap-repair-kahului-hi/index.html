Proper repair and maintenance are crucial for environmental protection.

    Regular grease trap cleaning and maintenance are the best preventive measures. Scrape food scraps into the trash before washing dishes, avoid pouring FOG down the drain, and consider installing strainers. Regular professional inspections can also catch small issues before they become major problems.

    Common signs include slow draining sinks, foul odors emanating from drains or the trap itself, backups in kitchen drains, or visible leaks around the grease trap unit. For businesses, frequent health code violations related to FOG can also indicate a failing trap.

    Grease traps are smaller, usually located inside or near the kitchen, and are suitable for low-volume establishments. Grease interceptors are much larger, often installed underground outside the building, and are designed to handle the high FOG volumes from large restaurants and commercial facilities. Both serve the same purpose of preventing FOGs from entering the sewer system.

    While minor clogs might seem DIY-fixable, grease trap repair is generally best left to licensed plumbers. Improper repairs can lead to greater damage, environmental fines, or health code violations for your business.
