    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Kailua, HI

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Kailua, HI

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer with a line to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. Behind the heater we can install moisture-resistant board and seal it.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Kailua

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Homes are commonly built on slabs or on post-and-pier foundations, and raised homes leave much of the plumbing exposed and easier to inspect. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Kailua, HI

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and run it to a sump basin so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. The difference is that the water no longer ends up on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and makes sense when a patio or driveway sits against the foundation.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Kailua, HI

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized to the water coming in, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed far enough out that it drains away from the house.

    Commercial Walls Waterproofing in Kailua

    Commercial basements, elevator pits and mechanical rooms in Kailua take on water the same way houses do, just with more at stake. One wet electrical room can close a business for days. We schedule around operating hours, work with property managers on access, and follow the waterproofing specs written into the building plans for below-grade walls. We keep downtime short.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Kailua

    Most jobs fall between $3,000 for interior crack sealing and $15,000 or more for exterior excavation with new footing drains. The size of the wall, how wet it gets, the method we pick and how easy the wall is to reach all move the number. Access matters most. Nothing starts until you have the quote in writing.

    Look low on the wall first. Damp patches, a musty smell, mold on the bottom of the drywall, a white powder on block or brick called efflorescence, and puddles after rain all point to water coming through. Stair-step cracks and bowing walls also deserve a closer look.

    It depends on the wall and the site. On exterior foundation work we use liquid polyurethane-modified asphalt, rubberized asphalt sheets, bentonite panels and crystalline coatings, picking the one that suits the soil and the wall material. Inside showers, it is Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban.

    Check the basement after a heavy rain. Wet spots on the walls, mold or mildew, a musty smell in the crawl space, efflorescence on concrete, new cracks in the foundation or standing water on the floor all mean water is getting in somewhere. Those signs usually get worse, not better.

    Sprinklers, nine times out of ten. Spray heads and drip lines aimed at the house soak the block every day, and soil sloped toward the wall or a short downspout adds to it, so the masonry stays wet and leaves mineral deposits as it dries. Sometimes adjusting a single sprinkler head solves it.

    Always. Sealing a wall with an active leak inside it traps the water, and the framing rots faster than it would have if you had left it open. The leak gets repaired and the cavity dried before anything else.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, generally more than about ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. We remove smaller affected areas ourselves.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to the method we use, the quality of the install and the soil conditions around your house. The sump pump will need replacing well before that. Plan on a new pump every seven to ten years.
