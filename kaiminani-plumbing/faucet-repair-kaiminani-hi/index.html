    Leaking Faucet Handle and Base Repair in Kaiminani, HI

    Water seeping around the handle or pooling at the base usually means failed O-rings on the spout or cartridge, or a mounting nut that has worked loose under the sink.     Pull-Down Kitchen Faucet Repair in Kaiminani

    Pull-down kitchen faucets wear at the hose, the spray head and the docking magnet or counterweight that pulls the hose back into the spout. Hoses split near the quick-connect. We replace hoses, spray heads and weights with brand parts, then check for drips under the sink where the hose connects.

    Tub Spout and Diverter Repair in Kaiminani, HI

    Water still running from the tub spout while the shower is on means the diverter is worn, either the gate inside the spout or a diverter stem in the valve. There are slip-on and threaded spouts. The pipe stub gets measured so we bring the right spout, and the fix gives you full pressure at the showerhead again.

    Disc Faucet Repair in Kaiminani

    Ceramic disc faucets last a long time, but grit from a water main repair or scale from hard water can scratch the discs or wear out the rubber inlet seals underneath. The seals are cheap. A scratched disc means a new cartridge, since the two discs are lapped to fit each other and cannot be resurfaced.

    Emergency Faucet Repair in Kaiminani

    A faucet that will not shut off, or one with a cracked body spraying under the sink, needs the water off before anything else happens. Turn the two small valves under the sink clockwise. If they will not turn, use the main shutoff for the house. We come fast for these.

    Kitchen Sprayer and Diverter Repair in Kaiminani, HI

    Spray heads on kitchen faucets get clogged with scale, and side sprayers lose pressure when the diverter inside the faucet body wears out. Diverters are cheap parts. Touch and touchless faucets add a solenoid and batteries to the list, and we test those before replacing anything.

    Dripping Faucet Repair in Kaiminani

    One drip a second adds up to more than 3,000 gallons a year, roughly the water used in 180 showers. That waste lands on your water bill. The worn washer or cartridge gets tracked down and replace it, then check the cabinet below for swelling or stains from the slow leak.
