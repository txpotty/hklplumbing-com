    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Kaiminani

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. We quote in writing after a site visit, with the trap, the labor and the permit fees listed as separate lines.

    It keeps cooking grease out of the public sewer. Inside the trap, warm wastewater slows and cools, so grease rises and stays put while water flows on. Without one, that grease hardens in the city mains and causes sewer overflows, and sewer authorities fine the kitchens they trace it to. Your own drain lines benefit too.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Sinks are calculated by compartment volume and drain-down time, typically one or two minutes, and dish machines by the manufacturer's discharge rating. That gives the GPM figure. Indoor units match the GPM, and outdoor tanks get sized by volume and retention.

    An under-sink hydromechanical trap with good access is usually a half-day job, including the flow control and a leak test. Floor-recessed units take longer because we cut and patch concrete. Outdoor tanks are different. A buried interceptor runs two to five working days after permit, mostly driven by the dig and the paving repair.

    Zurn and Watts make up a lot of our indoor installs, with Schier and Endura when a plastic body makes more sense. Outdoor interceptors are usually precast concrete, or polyethylene where a crane cannot reach. Any certified brand works. We skip uncertified units, since inspectors turn them down.

    Two warranties apply here. The trap body carries the maker's coverage, which runs from one year on some steel units to much longer on plastic. We cover our workmanship, meaning leaks at our joints and settling of a tank we set, and we give you the terms in writing before we start.

    Nearly every food business needs one. Codes require one wherever sinks or dish machines send greasy waste to the sewer.
