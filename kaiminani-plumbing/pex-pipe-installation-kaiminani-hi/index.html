    Uponor PEX System Installation in Kaiminani

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool plus a ring slipped over the pipe end. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.

    Hydronic Heating with PEX in Kaiminani

    Radiant floor loops are PEX too, though not the same pipe you drink from. Heating work calls for oxygen-barrier PEX, coated so oxygen cannot seep through the pipe wall and corrode the boiler, the circulator and the iron parts. Loop length matters as well. Too long and the far end runs cold.

    Commercial PEX Pipe Solutions in Kaiminani, HI

    Apartment buildings, small offices and churches in Kaiminani, HI are good candidates for PEX as much as houses can. With a manifold and shutoff for each unit, a leak in one apartment only takes that apartment offline, not the whole riser. Fire-rated walls need care. Where PEX passes through a fire-rated assembly, we use listed firestop sleeves or sealant so the rating holds.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Kaiminani

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Both last decades. In a cramped attic, we usually pick PEX-A.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Kaiminani, HI

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to swap out the old pipe. While the studs are exposed we can run PEX back to a manifold or a sound trunk, set fresh stub-outs for the sink and dishwasher, and add a fridge line. An addition gets sized on its own. A new bath at the far end of the house may need a three-quarter inch feed to keep pressure up.

    PEX for Water Line Replacement in Kaiminani

    Your service line runs from the meter or well to the house, usually 18 to 36 inches down. A PEX main comes off a coil in one piece, so there are no buried joints between the meter and the foundation wall. Some valleys and lowland areas have clay-rich soil that swells with heavy rain and can stress slabs, foundations, and buried pipes. We bed it in sand.
