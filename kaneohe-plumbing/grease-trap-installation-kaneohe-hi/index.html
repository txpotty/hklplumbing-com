    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Kaneohe

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. The air intake has to be vented to code, not left open in the kitchen. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. A trap that sits out of level loses capacity, so we shim it flat before connecting anything.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Kaneohe

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. You get a written, itemized number after we see the kitchen.

    Two warranties apply here. Manufacturer coverage on the unit varies by brand and material, and plastic bodies usually get the longer terms. We cover our workmanship, meaning leaks at our joints and settling of a tank we set, and we give you the terms in writing before we start.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. The trade usually calls the small indoor unit a trap and the big outdoor tank an interceptor. Codes vary on the wording. Some plumbing codes call both of them interceptors and separate them as hydromechanical and gravity, so we use whatever terms your permit office uses.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. Automatic grease removal units are a pricier option for kitchens that fry all day. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. We pick the rating first, then the brand that fits the space and budget.

    Plan on a permit. Both the building department and the sewer authority usually have a say. We file both when needed and give you copies of the approvals, which your health inspector may ask to see at opening. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    Zurn and Watts make up a lot of our indoor installs, with Schier and Endura when a plastic body makes more sense. Outdoor interceptors are usually precast concrete, or polyethylene where a crane cannot reach. Any certified brand works. What we will not install is an uncertified import trap, because inspectors reject them and the parts disappear after a year.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. A precast interceptor can last a very long time if it is pumped on schedule and the coating holds. Cleaning habits matter most. A trap that is scraped with a metal tool every week or left full of grease for months ages much faster than one that gets regular service.
