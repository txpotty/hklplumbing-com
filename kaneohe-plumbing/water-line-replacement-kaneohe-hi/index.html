    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Kaneohe, HI

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is thinner and mostly used inside the house. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against stone contact and soil chemistry. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Commercial Water Line Replacement in Kaneohe

    Larger buildings bring larger pipe, deeper trenches and more inspection steps. Backflow testing and the report to the water provider are part of the job. Trench shoring comes into play below five feet, and we plan the work so the outage lasts hours instead of days, often running a temporary supply while the new line is tied in. Night work is possible.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Kaneohe, HI

    Bursting fits old cast iron or galvanized lines that will shatter under the head. Copper and poly are better pulled than burst. Either way the new pipe follows the old path, which matters when the route runs under a patio or a neighbor's fence, and the yard work shrinks to two pits. Rock can stop it.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Kaneohe

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? A repair coupling is often the right call. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so the full line costs less than a string of repairs. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Kaneohe, HI

    Pipe pulling works like this. Two pits get dug, one at each end. A cable or bursting head goes through the old pipe and drags new HDPE or PEX back through the same path, splitting the old pipe if it is brittle, so a driveway, a row of shrubs or a concrete walk can stay where it is.

    Trenchless Bores Under Driveways in Kaneohe

    Directional boring drills a path under the obstacle and pulls the pipe back through it. No old line is needed to follow. We use it when the new line has to take a different route, say around a new addition or under a driveway poured after the house was built, and the launch and exit pits stay small. Heavy rock is the exception.

    Copper Service Line Installation in Kaneohe, HI

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. Plenty of copper mains have lasted 60 years. We flare or use brass compression fittings at the ends, keep the run in one piece between them, and pack sand around it so rocks do not touch the pipe. Where it goes through a concrete wall, it gets a plastic sleeve and a seal to keep water and bugs out.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Kaneohe, HI

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Frozen pipes are essentially a non-issue for homes in Hawaii, since sea-level and most residential areas never get cold enough to freeze water lines. PEX can swell a little in a freeze and go back to shape, where copper splits. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and we keep it out of direct sun at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.
