    Cost-Effective Waterproofing Solutions in Kihei, HI

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation.     Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Kihei

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests. We inject them with low-pressure polyurethane, which expands to fill the crack from the inside face to the soil side, or with epoxy when the crack also needs structural bonding. Horizontal cracks are different. A horizontal crack often signals soil pressure pushing the wall in, so we check for movement with a level before we seal anything. Vertical cracks are usually a one-visit job.

    Drainage System Integration in Kihei, HI

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Exterior footing drains, interior drain tile and surface French drains all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a gutter dumping at the corner. Downspouts get extended at least four feet out.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Kihei

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin starting at the lowest port and working up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane flexes a bit if the wall shifts, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Kihei

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, cement board with a membrane or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Commercial Wall Waterproofing in Kihei

    Restaurant kitchens, gym locker rooms and salon wash stations put water on walls all day. We waterproof commercial showers, mop sink walls and restroom wet walls with membranes listed for continuous wet use, and we repair the supply and drain piping behind them if demolition turns up a leak. After-hours scheduling is available.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Kihei

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Homes are commonly built on slabs or on post-and-pier foundations, and raised homes keep much of the plumbing exposed and easier to inspect. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Kihei, HI

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized for the inflow, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed at least 10 feet from the house.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Kihei

    Shower wall waterproofing with a membrane system usually runs $1,200 to $3,500 including demolition and backer board, but not counting the new tile. Block wall sealing outside often costs $800 to $3,000. Foundation waterproofing below grade is usually $2,500 to $8,000.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but will not stop water that is pushing against the wall under pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, which is what a basement in wet soil or a high water table needs.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, digging equipment, proper membrane thickness and knowing how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. A bad install can trap water against the wall and make things worse.

    Check the basement after a heavy rain. Wet spots on the walls, mold or mildew, a musty smell in the crawl space, new foundation cracks, efflorescence on the concrete or standing water on the floor all mean water is getting in somewhere. Those signs rarely go away on their own.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, including the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Bigger foundation jobs take several days, sometimes more if it rains.

    Every install gets a written warranty on materials and workmanship. Your proposal lists exactly what is covered and for how long, and when a manufacturer offers its own warranty on a membrane or resin we register it in your name. Keep the paperwork with your house records.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, using drain tile under the slab edge and a sump pump. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. Outside costs more.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to how it was installed, what method was used and what the soil around the house is like. Sump pumps wear out sooner. Plan on a new pump every seven to ten years.
