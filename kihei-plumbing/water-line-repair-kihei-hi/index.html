    Slab Leak Water Line Repair in Kihei

    A slab leak is a supply pipe leaking under the concrete floor of the house. Older homes may still have galvanized steel supply pipes or cast iron drains, both of which corrode faster in a warm, salty, humid environment. Signs include a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water and a meter that moves with everything off. Acoustic gear and a thermal camera tell us where it is. From there you have three choices: break the slab at the spot, reroute the line through the walls or attic, or skip the old under-slab pipe and repipe overhead with PEX. You get a price on every option.

    Water Line Repair at the Meter in Kihei, HI

    The meter box is where a lot of leaks start. Compression fittings there work loose over time. Before we touch anything, we figure out whether the leak is before or after the meter, because the water provider owns the meter and everything on the street side. If it is ours to fix, we fix it. That often means new fittings and a new customer shutoff valve on the same visit.

    Water Pressure Restoration in Kihei

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. First step is a pressure gauge on the outside spigot. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the house shutoff and the meter-side valve for a partly closed gate, and look at the pipe itself. Galvanized pipe can lose half its opening to rust scale. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    After-Hours Water Line Breaks in Kihei, HI

    Water lines tend to break on a Saturday night. We answer around the clock. HKL Plumbing sends a plumber who can shut off the curb stop, pump the hole and make a permanent repair on the spot, which saves you from a weekend on a temporary patch. If the break is on the utility side of the meter, we tell you right away and help you get the provider out. That happens more than you would think.

    Main Water Line Repair in Kihei

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. A leak here shows up as a soggy strip of lawn and a meter dial that never rests. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. A moving dial with the house valve closed means an underground leak. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, and we dig one hole instead of a trench across your yard.

    Main Water Line Leak Repair in Kihei

    A meter that moves with every fixture off is the clearest sign you have. We confirm it in about ten minutes. The house shutoff gets closed, the dial gets watched, and continued movement tells us the leak is in the buried line. Ground microphones find the spot. A repair coupling or a new length of pipe goes in, and then we pressure test it.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Kihei

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. Then the new pipe gets pulled through underground. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, and most jobs wrap up in a single day.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Kihei, HI

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, which can hold for a while if the pipe is not too far gone. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Kihei

    Price depends mostly on depth, access and pipe material. Shallow spot repairs cost the least. Most single-leak repairs land between $300 and $800, while a full main line replacement can run $1,500 to $6,000 or more depending on length and what sits on top of the pipe. You get a written estimate after we locate the leak.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, 811 marking, safe digging around the gas and power lines nearby and code-approved fittings made up correctly. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    Usually not. The digging is the risky part. Gas, power and fiber can run right alongside a water line, and hitting one with a shovel or a trenching tool can hurt you badly and cost a lot to fix. Joining pipe underground also takes the right fittings and tools, plus a pressure test before backfill.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. Listen at the meter box for hissing. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. If it moves with everything off, you have a leak.

    Spot repairs usually take a few hours. Plan on a day for a full replacement. Jobs with a deep line, a lot of hardscape or a trenchless pull can stretch to two or three days, mostly because of restoration work and inspection timing. We give you a time estimate with the quote.

    A typical spot repair runs two to four hours, including the locate and backfill. Replacements take longer. Deep lines, long runs and jobs under concrete or pavers can push the job into a second or third day. You get a realistic timeline when we hand you the quote.

    A full service line replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. Small repairs often do not. The rules come from your city or county, so the answer can change from one town to the next. We pull the permit and schedule the inspection when it is required.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.
