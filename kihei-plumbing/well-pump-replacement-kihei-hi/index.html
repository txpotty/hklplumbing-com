    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Kihei

    The cost of well pump replacement in Kihei, HI can vary widely, usually ranging from $1,500 to $5,000 or more. This price depends on factors like the type of pump, submersible or jet, well depth, horsepower and any additional components needed like pressure tanks or control boxes. We provide a written, upfront estimate after diagnosing your situation.

    Shallow well pumps, or jet pumps, are designed for wells where the water level is typically 25 feet or less from the pump, using suction to draw water. Deep well pumps, or submersible pumps, are placed directly in the water near the bottom of the well and push water up, suited for depths over 25 feet. Choosing the correct type is key for efficiency and performance.

    Most standard well pump replacements can be completed within a single day, typically 4 to 8 hours. However, complex installations involving very deep wells, difficult access or additional system repairs may take longer. Our team works quickly to restore your water as soon as possible.

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up from shallower wells, usually less than 25 feet. Submersible pumps are placed directly inside the well casing, submerged in water, and are used for deeper wells, pushing water up to the surface. Each has its own applications and benefits.

    New well pumps usually come with a manufacturer's warranty, usually ranging from 1 to 5 years, covering defects in materials and workmanship. HKL Plumbing also stands behind our installation work with our own labor warranty. We will discuss warranty details for your chosen pump during the quoting process.

    Well pump replacement is a complex job that involves high voltage electricity, heavy equipment and possibly deep well access. It requires specialized tools and experience for proper installation, electrical safety and compliance with local plumbing codes. It is highly recommended to hire a licensed professional to avoid injury, property damage or costly mistakes.

    Common signs of a failing well pump include no water, sputtering faucets, reduced water pressure, discolored water or unusually high electricity bills. If your pump runs constantly or cycles on and off too frequently, these are also signs that replacement may be necessary.

    Most well pumps last 8 to 15 years. Sediment, hard water and frequent cycling can shorten lifespan.
