    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Makakilo

    A single clogged sink or tub in Makakilo, HI usually runs $200 to $350. Main sewer lines with roots or jetting tend to land between $400 and $1,000. Location changes the price. A clean outdoor cleanout keeps the price down, while pulling a toilet or working through a roof vent adds time. You get the number before we start.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting is different. A jetter scours the walls with water at high pressure. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. Hydro jetting sends water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe wall as it is pulled back toward the cleanout. Jetting leaves the pipe cleaner. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. Cables can kink, whip and wrap around a wrist in a second. They also get stuck. A rental cable stuck in an old clay line can turn a $300 rooter call into a dig to retrieve it.

    Gurgling toilets, sewage odors and slow drains in several fixtures are the common signs. Water backing up into a tub when you flush is another clue. That one is serious. Frequent clogs in the same line also point to a deeper blockage, often roots at a joint 30 or 40 feet out from the house.

    Clearing a clog in Makakilo does not need a permit. Repairs are another matter. A new cleanout, a dig to fix broken pipe or a sewer replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. We pull it and schedule the inspection for you.

    Keep grease, coffee grounds and wipes out of drains. Put strainers in tub and shower drains to catch hair. After using the disposal, run hot water for a bit and scrape food into the trash first to keep the kitchen line clearer. Root-prone lines need cutting every year or two.

    Rooting is safe with a trained tech and the correct cable and blade. Badly cracked or collapsed pipe can be harmed by any cleaning method. The tech's touch matters. If the cable tells us the pipe is damaged, we stop and send in a camera before going further.
