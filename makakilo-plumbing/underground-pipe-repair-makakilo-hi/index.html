    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Makakilo

    A single repair on a buried water line typically costs $800 to $2,500 including locating and excavation. A new service runs higher. Main water service replacement typically runs $2,500 to $6,500, while sewer lateral repairs usually fall between $1,500 and $5,000 based on depth. You get the price on paper.

    Less digging is the big one. The yard stays mostly intact. Trenchless methods leave driveways, patios and established landscaping alone, and the savings on putting the yard back often cover the higher method price. It is often faster.

    We use PEX from major manufacturers like Uponor and Viega. Everything is code-listed. PVC and ABS products meet the ASTM specs for buried water or drain service, and fittings are rated for direct burial so they hold up to soil contact and moisture for decades. We skip cheap parts.

    Shallow irrigation leaks are sometimes a fair DIY job. Service lines are different. Main water lines and sewer laterals require locating, utility marks and code-compliant fittings rated for burial, so those should go to a licensed plumber. Utilities have to be marked before digging.

    In most cities, the homeowner owns that line. The utility owns the meter. The utility usually maintains the meter and the pipe on the street side, and we can help confirm which side of the meter the leak is on before anyone digs. It matters for the bill.

    For water lines we use PEX or Type K copper. Both are rated for burial. Sewer and drain work uses Schedule 40 PVC, and trenchless jobs use fused HDPE, and every one of these holds up to soil and water far better than galvanized or clay. Local code decides the final pick.

    A minor leak repair can take a few hours. Bigger jobs run a day or more. A full trenchless sewer replacement could take one to three days, with footage and inspection timing as the main variables. The timeline is in the estimate.

    Yes, every common material. Old and new. PVC, ABS, cast iron, copper, PEX, clay, galvanized and polybutylene all come up, and each one needs its own transition fitting when we join new pipe to the old. Common fittings ride on the truck.
