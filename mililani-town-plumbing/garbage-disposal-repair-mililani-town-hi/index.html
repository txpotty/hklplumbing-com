    What Fails on a Disposal, and How We Fix It in Mililani Town, HI

    Grinding Noise Repair in Mililani Town

    Loud metal-on-metal sounds may mean a loose mount, a foreign object or worn impellers. After checking the chamber, snugging the mount and pulling debris, we run it empty to hear if the noise is coming from the motor. Bad bearings mean replacement.

    Flange and Inlet Leak Repair in Mililani Town, HI

    Leaks show up at the flange, dishwasher inlet, discharge tube or body. We trace the leak, reseal the flange with plumber's putty and replace gaskets or tubes as needed. Water from the bottom usually means a failed internal seal, which makes replacement the smarter move. It is not a repairable part.

    Noisy Disposal Repair in Mililani Town

    Grinding, rattling or a hum that sounds rough usually mean trouble inside the unit. Loose mounting rings rattle too. We check the mount first, then the chamber for debris, then listen to the motor with the chamber empty, because a motor that whines with nothing in it has bad bearings.

    Disposal Reset and Electrical Repair in Mililani Town

    If a disposal is completely dead, the thermal overload may have tripped or the switch may be bad. We check the reset button, breaker, wall switch and wiring, and we also test air switches and batch-feed lids that use a magnet to start the motor. Most are simple fixes.

    Preventive Care and Longevity in Mililani Town, HI

    Keep cold water running before, during and after use, and let it go about 15 seconds after the motor stops so the food clears the trap. Grease poured in with hot water coats the chamber and the pipe, while cold water keeps it solid so the impellers can break it up. Fibrous scraps jam it most. Throw out celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.

    Slow Draining Disposal Repair in Mililani Town, HI

    Fine grinding with slow draining usually means the discharge tube or the trap arm is packed. Rice and coffee grounds are the usual culprits. We pull the trap, clear the tube and the arm with the right size cable, and check that the arm has at least a quarter inch of fall per foot toward the wall.

    Humming Disposal Repair in Mililani Town

    Humming with no spin almost always means a jam. Bones, pits, glass and spoons are the usual suspects. We cut power, work the flywheel free with a hex wrench on the bottom of the unit, remove the object and then test the motor and the reset button with water running.

    Jammed Disposal Repair in Mililani Town

    A jam usually comes from one hard object, like a peach pit, a chicken bone or a twist tie wrapped around the impeller. We free the flywheel from below with the hex key and pull the object with pliers, never a hand, then run it with water to make sure nothing else is stuck. We press the reset after clearing it. Check the trap arm if it jams again.
