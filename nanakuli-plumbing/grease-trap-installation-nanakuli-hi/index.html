    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Nanakuli

    Installed prices for small indoor hydromechanical traps typically fall between $1,500 and $5,000. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more once excavation and site restoration are counted. Brand is a small part of it. We give you a written estimate after we walk the site, and it does not change unless we hit something underground nobody could see.

    Coverage comes from two places. Your trap carries a factory warranty that varies by brand and material. Our written labor warranty covers the joints we made and how we set the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. The trade usually calls the small indoor unit a trap and the big outdoor tank an interceptor. Codes vary on the wording. Some plumbing codes call both of them interceptors and separate them as hydromechanical and gravity, so we use whatever terms your permit office uses.

    On a commercial kitchen, no. The work needs a licensed plumber and a permit in nearly every jurisdiction. A homeowner adding a small under-sink trap to a home kitchen may be allowed to do it, but even then the flow control and vent have to be right or the trap siphons and does nothing. Ask the building department first.

    Pump-outs every couple of weeks on a trap that used to go two months is the clearest sign you have outgrown it or the baffles have failed. So is a sour grease smell near the floor sinks. Rust shows up too. We also look downstream, because grease in the outlet pipe means the trap is letting it through.

    An under-sink hydromechanical trap with good access is usually a half-day job, including the flow control and a leak test. Recessing one into the slab adds a day or two for saw-cutting and patching. Outdoor tanks are different. A buried interceptor runs two to five working days after permit, mostly driven by the dig and the paving repair.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. Factory terms vary, so we hand you the registration card or file it for you. Keep your cleaning logs. Makers may ask for service records before paying a claim.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. A precast interceptor can last a very long time if it is pumped on schedule and the coating holds. Cleaning habits matter most. A trap that is scraped with a metal tool every week or left full of grease for months ages much faster than one that gets regular service.
