    What Goes Into a Sewer Lateral Replacement in Nanakuli, HI

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Nanakuli

    Cast iron rots from the inside out. The bottom of a cast iron pipe goes first, and the camera will show a trench rusted through even when the top looks fine. Rough scale catches everything. Outside the foundation we replace it with PVC or HDPE. Under-slab cast iron gets a separate look at lining or rerouting.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Nanakuli

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Clay joints let roots in every few feet, cast iron scales and rusts through along the bottom, and Orangeburg tar paper pipe slowly flattens into an oval. Modern plastic pipe avoids all three problems. Fused HDPE has no joints at all, so roots have nowhere to start.

    Fixing a Bellied Sewer by Trench in Nanakuli, HI

    A belly is a low spot that holds water. Trenchless methods ride the old pipe, sag and all, which means a real belly has to be dug out and relaid on grade. Rocky ground slows the dig. We price that in after the locate shows depth. Joints are inspected by us before the city does.

    Spot Sewer Repairs in Nanakuli, HI

    Sometimes one joint is the whole problem. With only one bad section, we excavate a single small pit and splice in new PVC using shielded couplings. That costs much less. We only suggest it when the footage backs it up.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Nanakuli

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. We open a trench along the old line, pull the broken pipe and lay new PVC on compacted bedding at a steady fall of an eighth to a quarter inch per foot. Sod gets cut and rolled aside first when the lawn allows it. The inspector sees open pipe. We compact the backfill in layers, which keeps a dip from forming along the trench.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Nanakuli

    No replacement quote without video. The camera goes in at the cleanout and travels out to the main, recording every crack, offset and root ball as it goes. A sonde in the camera head lets us mark the depth on the lawn. The footage tells us whether one section or the whole line needs to go.

    Keeping a New Sewer Line Healthy in Nanakuli

    New pipe still needs a little care. Keep willows, silver maples and other thirsty trees well away from the sewer path, because even a good line can get stressed by a large root system pushing on it. Much of Hawaii sits on volcanic rock and lava, which makes trenching for sewer and water lines slow and sometimes requires special equipment. Wipes do not break down. On older homes, a quick camera look every few years spots trouble at the tap before it backs up.
