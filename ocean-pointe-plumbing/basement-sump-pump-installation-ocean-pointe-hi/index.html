    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. We check the float, clean the pit and test the check valve, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. It takes under an hour.

    For some homeowners, a same-model pump swap is a fair weekend job. Cutting a new pit or adding a backup is not, because those mistakes end in flooding, cracked slabs or electrical hazards, and fixing them costs more than paying for the install up front. Those belong to a pro.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. A pedestal mounts the motor on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Either type gets the job done.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner.     Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.
