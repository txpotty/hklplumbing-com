    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Ocean Pointe

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit typically runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    We recommend having your sump pump inspected and maintained once a year, ideally before the rainy season. Regular maintenance includes checking the float switch, cleaning debris out of the pit, testing the check valve and making sure the discharge line is clear. This helps prevent unexpected failures and extends the life of your pump.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, designed to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is usually not allowed by local codes.

    Copper was the standard for many years, while newer homes and remodels in Hawaii increasingly use PEX, which resists corrosion from salt air. Homes built on slabs without basements rarely need one. Homes that do have basements or below-grade rooms can take on water quickly during heavy storms, especially where the soil sheds rain instead of absorbing it.

    We install a wide range of high-quality, reliable sump pump brands, including Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps. We can help you choose the best brand and model for your budget and water management needs in your Ocean Pointe home, considering horsepower and features.

    A submersible sump pump sits entirely within the sump pit, making it quieter and less obtrusive. A pedestal sump pump has its motor above the pit, making it easier to access for maintenance and usually more affordable. Both types are effective for removing water from your basement.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump every 2 to 3 months, especially before and after the rainy season. Simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit to make sure the pump activates, drains the water and shuts off properly. Regular testing helps identify issues early.
