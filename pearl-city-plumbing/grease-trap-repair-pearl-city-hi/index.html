    Baffle Repair and Replacement in Pearl City

    Inside a concrete gravity interceptor the baffle is usually a poured or precast wall with a crossover opening. A crumbling wall lets grease slip into the second compartment and out the outlet. We can rebuild the wall with form work and a sulfate-resistant mix or set a polyethylene baffle panel, depending on the tank. Both require a full pump-out and a dry tank.

    Grease Line Clog Repair in Pearl City, HI

    Lines downstream of the trap should stay clean if the trap is working. When we find grease past the outlet, the trap is not doing its job.     Damaged Baffle Repair in Pearl City

    Baffles are the walls inside the trap that slow the water down long enough for grease to float and solids to sink. A cracked or loose baffle turns the trap into a pass-through. We pump it out and pull the old baffle. Plastic units get a factory replacement part. Steel traps with a rusted-out baffle may need a fabricated piece, and sometimes a new trap is the cheaper answer.

    Flow Control Device Repair in Pearl City

    Hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, like 20 or 50 gallons per minute, and the flow control fitting is what holds the water to that rate. Without it, a full sink dump pushes grease straight through. Inspectors look for it. The air intake gets cleared too, because the fitting needs air to work.

    Inlet and Outlet Tee Repair in Pearl City, HI

    Tees are cheap parts with an outsized job. On the inlet side, the tee directs incoming water down under the floating grease. The outlet tee draws water from low in the tank, well under the grease cap, so if it breaks off or someone removes it during pumping, floating grease walks right out to the sewer. New tees go in as Schedule 40 PVC, cut to the drop length the manufacturer or local code specifies.

    Grease Interceptor Clog Removal in Pearl City

    Grease does not stay liquid for long once it cools in a pipe. It sets up like candle wax on the crown of the line. Chemical drain openers soften it for a day and then it re-forms farther downstream, sometimes past the trap entirely, where it becomes a sewer problem instead of a kitchen problem. A jetter actually takes it out of the line.
