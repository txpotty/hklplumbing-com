    Frequently Asked Questions About Drainage Services in Royal Kunia

    Drain clearing in Royal Kunia usually runs $125 to $500 depending on the line, and hydro jetting runs $350 to $800. Yard drains cost more. French drain and downspout jobs usually land between $2,000 and $8,000, based on length and hardscape.

    Snaking is the right first step for most single clogs. It costs less and takes less time. Jetting wins for grease, scale and recurring roots since it cleans the whole wall.

    Roots usually enter through an existing crack or joint, then widen it as they grow. Clay pipe can split over time. A root mass can also push a joint out of line, creating an offset that catches waste, and in bad cases the pipe collapses and has to be dug up or lined from the inside.

    Drain cleaning usually means a cable machine with a cutting head that bores through the clog. It works fast and costs less. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the whole pipe wall clean of grease, scale and roots, so it lasts longer but costs more and needs a pipe that is in good enough shape to take the pressure.

    A typical French drain or downspout system takes one to three days. Bigger jobs take longer. Dry wells, long trenches or saw-cut concrete can push it toward a week.

    Mineral scale inside pipes narrows them and traps grease, hair and soap. Most municipal water in Hawaii comes from volcanic groundwater aquifers and is generally soft to moderate, so heavy scale is less of a problem than in many places. Older galvanized and cast iron drains are the worst, because their rough interior gives buildup something to cling to, and every new layer narrows the pipe a little more. A jetter clears the buildup.

    Roots grow toward the water and nutrients inside sewer pipe. A hairline crack is enough. A small root at a cracked joint becomes a mass that traps paper and grease, especially in clay pipe.

    Watch for water pooling near the house after storms. Washed-out mulch tells you too. Inside, a damp crawlspace, a musty basement or efflorescence on the foundation wall all mean water is reaching the house instead of being carried away, and the problem usually starts at a downspout or a low spot.
