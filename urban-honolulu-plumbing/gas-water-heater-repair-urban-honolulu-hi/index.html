    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Urban Honolulu

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. No flame at all usually means a plugged pilot orifice or a bad piezo. Main burner problems show up another way. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. We test each piece in that order.

    Burner Tray and Venting Repair in Urban Honolulu

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. We pull the burner, clear the ports and look at the flame arrestor screen underneath. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Urban Honolulu home. We hold a smoke pencil at the draft hood to see which way the air moves.

    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Urban Honolulu

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Some rural homes in Hawaii rely on rainwater catchment, which needs good filtration and disinfection to keep tanks and fixtures in safe shape. The tech pulls the anode, checks what metal remains and swaps it if bare core wire shows. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Urban Honolulu home that has not started leaking from the shell. Water from the shell seam is another story. That tank needs replacing.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Urban Honolulu

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. Whistling points somewhere else. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.

    Repairing Older Gas Water Heaters in Urban Honolulu

    Older tanks with standing pilots and no vapor sensor are still out there in Urban Honolulu, mostly in basements and utility closets. We can keep a pre-2003 heater running when the tank is sound, but we also check the draft hood, the flue connector and the gas cock, since those fittings on an old install are often original and corroded. Some old setups lack a sediment trap. We add one during the repair.
