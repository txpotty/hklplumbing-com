    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Urban Honolulu

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically runs $125 to $300. Main sewer rooting through a cleanout usually runs $200 to $500. Adding a jetting pass brings the total to about $450 to $1,000, depending on line length and how much grease is in it. After-hours calls cost more.

    Watch for very slow drains, gurgling from toilets and sour odors. Water rising in a tub or shower when another fixture drains is a strong sign of a main line clog. That one is urgent. Waiting raises the odds that the next flush puts sewage on the floor.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Urban Honolulu's established homes take the most damage. They also put our techs at risk. Let us know if you used a chemical, because that water splashes when the trap comes off.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. A root-choked main or a long jetting job may take 3 to 6 hours. You get a time estimate first. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They also burn skin. A rooter cable removes the clog itself rather than eating a channel through it, so the line stays open far longer.

    Every rooter job from HKL Plumbing carries a guarantee for a set period on the line we cleared. The terms are explained before work begins. Coverage is specific. If that same line stops up again within the guarantee window, we come back and clear it at no extra charge.

    A limited guarantee for a set number of days comes with most rooter work. If the line clogs again within that time, we return at no extra charge.

    Most single-drain clogs take 1 to 3 hours from arrival to flow test. Main lines with heavy roots, or jobs that need a jetting pass, can stretch to half a day or more. Access is the wild card. A buried or missing cleanout means pulling a toilet first, which adds about an hour.
