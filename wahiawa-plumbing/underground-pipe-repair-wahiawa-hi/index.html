    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Wahiawa

    A single repair on a buried water line usually costs $800 to $2,500 including locating and excavation. Full main water service replacement typically runs $2,500 to $6,500, and sewer lateral repairs range from $1,500 to $5,000 depending on depth.

    Common signs include a water bill that jumps without explanation, a meter that moves with all water off, low pressure and wet or sinking spots in the yard. A hissing sound near the meter box can also signal a leak.

    We use acoustic listening devices that pick up the sound of escaping water, tracer gas that rises through soil at the leak point and electronic locators that trace the pipe path. Together these narrow the leak to a small area.

    Common signs of a broken underground pipe include unexplained wet spots or lush patches in your yard, a sudden drop in water pressure, unusual odors such as sulfur or sewage, gurgling sounds from drains or toilets or an unexpected increase in your water bill. If you notice any of these, it's smart to call for a professional inspection promptly.

    The length of an underground pipe repair depends heavily on the severity and location of the damage, as well as the repair method chosen. Minor repairs using trenchless techniques might be completed in a single day. More extensive issues, such as full sewer line replacements requiring excavation, could take two to five days, including restoration work.

    Whether an underground pipe leak is covered by insurance depends on your homeowner's policy and the cause of the damage. Typically, sudden and accidental damage may be covered, but issues from normal wear and tear or neglect are generally not. It's best to review your policy or contact your insurance provider directly for details.

    Trenchless pipe repair offers several key advantages, including minimal disruption to your yard, landscaping and concrete, which can save on restoration costs. It's usually faster than traditional excavation and less invasive, preserving the look and value of your property. This method is also durable, often extending the lifespan of your pipes.
