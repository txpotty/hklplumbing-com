    Hydro-Jetting for Grease Lines in Waianae, HI

    A cable machine pokes a hole through grease. Jetting actually removes it. Our jetters run around 3,000 to 4,000 psi through a spinning nozzle, which scours the whole inside of the pipe and pushes the loosened grease downstream. A camera pass afterward shows whether the line is really clean.

    Grease Line Jetting in Waianae

    Grease that slips past a full trap coats the 4-inch line out to the main. It builds up in rings, thickest at the bottom of the pipe. A snake cuts a narrow path through and the line plugs again within weeks, while a jetter with a rotating nozzle peels the grease off the whole pipe wall. We run a camera after to confirm.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Waianae, HI

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. Busy kitchens need them cleaned every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. Most odor from these units comes from a bad gasket.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Waianae

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. Each compartment gets pumped separately. A hard grease cap gets broken up with a pressure wand before pumping, otherwise it plugs the suction hose and half of it stays in the tank. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.

    Preventive Maintenance Programs in Waianae, HI

    A maintenance plan is just a pumping interval plus someone who tracks it. We keep the calendar so your manager does not have to. If the sludge judge shows the trap hitting 20 percent between visits, we pull the next date in, and if it reads 10 percent, we push it out and save you a pump-out. Adjustments happen at every visit.

    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in Waianae, HI

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. A 20-gallon under-sink trap is a short visit. A 1,500-gallon interceptor left five months needs a truck, a pressure wand and disposal fees by the gallon, which puts it at the top of the range. A steady schedule lowers what each visit costs.
