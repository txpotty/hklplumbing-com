    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Waipahu

    Costs range from about $200 for simple thawing to $1,500 or more for a burst pipe inside a finished wall. Access drives the price. A frozen line in an open crawlspace is quick work, while a split pipe in a second-floor ceiling means opening drywall, repairing the pipe and leaving the opening ready for a drywall patch.

    Most thawing jobs take one to two hours. Fixing a burst usually takes a few hours. Repairs inside finished ceilings or walls take longer because we open the drywall, repair the pipe, test it and leave the opening clean and ready for a patch, and multiple splits on one run add time as well.

    Water heater swaps, repiping, new fixtures, and any work tied to a cesspool or septic upgrade typically require a permit. Most codes call for protecting pipes in unheated spaces and limit supply lines in outside walls. We build to the local edition. Inspectors check it.

    Repairs carry a written workmanship warranty, with the term on your invoice. Manufacturer warranties cover parts. The warranty covers our repair, not a new freeze, so if the same line freezes again because it is still exposed, we will talk with you about insulation, heat cable or rerouting so it does not keep happening.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. The outside part drains when you close it. It only works if the hose is disconnected, because a connected hose traps water in the stem where it can freeze and split the valve inside the wall, which you may not notice until spring.

    A short, exposed section usually thaws in 30 to 60 minutes. Pipes inside walls take more time. Lines in walls or plastic pipe can take a few hours with gentler heat.

    Insulate exposed pipes in attics, crawlspaces and garages, and seal air leaks around them. Disconnect garden hoses before cold weather. On the coldest nights, let a faucet on an outside wall drip, open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls, and keep the thermostat at the same setting overnight instead of turning it down.

    A faucet that trickles or runs dry after a cold night is the most common sign. Visible frost on a pipe confirms it. You may also notice a toilet that will not refill, a bulge in a pipe, or a strange smell from a drain, and any of those means you should open the faucet and call before the line thaws on its own.
