    Inlet and Outlet Baffle Replacement in Waipahu, HI

    Baffles hold back the scum layer on top and the sludge on the bottom so only clear effluent leaves the tank. Concrete ones get eaten by sulfuric acid from sewer gas and eventually fall off into the tank. Once the outlet baffle is gone, solids head straight for the field. We replace them with PVC sanitary tees. Usually a short job.

    Septic Pipe Repair in Waipahu, HI

    Old homes may still have clay tile or Orangeburg pipe running to the tank, and both fail badly with age. Much of Hawaii sits on volcanic rock and lava, which makes trenching for sewer and water lines slow and sometimes requires special equipment. Roots and settling ground split joints and crush the pipe. We replace the bad run with 4-inch PVC, set on sand bedding with a quarter inch per foot of fall. Short sections take an afternoon.

    Aerobic and Alternative System Repair in Waipahu, HI

    Aerobic treatment units use an air pump to feed oxygen to the bacteria, and when that pump dies the unit starts to smell within days. Our techs rebuild linear aerators and swap rotary ones, plus clean out diffusers clogged with solids. Timers and panels get tested too. We also keep the service log your permit may require.

    Drain Field Line Jetting in Waipahu

    Distribution laterals clog from the inside with solids and slime, especially after years without pumping. A jetter goes into each lateral from the D-box or an end cleanout, and the water that comes back tells us how bad it is. Roots get cut. If the soil itself has sealed, jetting will not fix it, and we say so.

    Effluent Pump Replacement in Waipahu, HI

    Effluent pumps in a dosing chamber last roughly 8 to 15 years, and they usually quit without much warning at all. A pump that hums but moves no water has a stuck impeller or a bad capacitor. Our tech pulls the old unit and checks the discharge before the new one goes in. Floats get replaced at the same time.

    Septic Line Clog Clearing in Waipahu

    A slow toilet and a gurgle in the tub tend to mean something is stuck in the line before the tank. We run a camera down the line first, then clear it with a jetter sized for 4-inch sewer pipe. Grease and wipes cause most of these, and what we do next depends on what comes out of the pipe. Roots mean a joint has opened.
