    Slab Leak Repiping in Waipio

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Homes are commonly built on slabs or on post-and-pier foundations, and raised homes keep much of the plumbing exposed and easier to inspect. Instead of breaking concrete to patch one pipe, we reroute new supply lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, then abandon the old lines in the slab. No jackhammer needed. It ends this leak and takes the rest of the slab plumbing out of the picture.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Waipio

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. Rerouting one branch overhead and leaving the bad pipe in the slab is often cheaper than both a repipe and a jackhammer repair. It suits a first-time leak. If the house has had several leaks, we will walk through why a full repipe usually costs less over time.

    PEX Repiping in Waipio, HI

    PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run long distances without a fitting, which means fewer joints hidden in your walls. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. It resists scale and chlorine, handles a freeze better than copper and costs less to install, which is why most of our repipes use it. Our PEX-A installs use expansion fittings that do not shrink the flow path. Flow stays strong at the last shower.

    PEX Pipe Replacement in Waipio, HI

    PEX tubing resists freezing damage, corrosion and scale better than most metal pipe. It is quieter too. Flexible PEX soaks up some of the pressure spike behind water hammer, and its long fitting-free runs leave fewer weak points. We keep PEX out of sunlight and away from water heater flues.

    Drain Line Repiping in Waipio

    Drain pipe wears out too, especially old cast iron that cracks and scales up inside. Slow drains in several fixtures, sewer odors and repeated clogs in the same line are common signs of a failing drain system. A camera shows the condition. New PVC or ABS, sloped and vented properly, replaces the bad sections and drains better than the old pipe.

    Copper Repiping in Waipio, HI

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings. Expect a slightly longer job. Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    Emergency Pipe Failures and Repipes in Waipio

    Sometimes a house reaches the repipe point all at once, with a burst line and two more leaks right behind it. We stop the active leak first, make temporary repairs that get water back on and then plan the full repipe so you are not rushed into it. That gives you time. Insurance may cover the water damage from the burst even if it does not cover the repipe itself, so we document what we find with photos.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Waipio

    Most whole-home repipes in Waipio, HI fall somewhere between $4,000 and $15,000. Bigger homes cost more. Square footage, fixture count, material choice and the amount of drywall work along the route set the final price. The quote comes in writing.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. It helps at sale time. Swapping out polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized makes a home easier to insure and sell, especially with a permit record and transferable warranty.

    Most repipes in Waipio, HI take three to five working days for an average house. Larger homes run longer. Copper adds time because each turn needs a fitting, and patching usually takes one more day after the plumbing passes.

    A repipe needs a permit, specialized tools, code knowledge and a passing pressure test. It is not a weekend job. Hidden leaks, failed inspections or a voided pipe warranty from a DIY job can easily cost more than a licensed crew would have.

    You get a written workmanship warranty covering labor and materials. Details are spelled out up front. Our repipes use Uponor PEX-A or domestic Type L copper, and certified PEX-A jobs also get the manufacturer's long-term warranty.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX costs less. PEX bends, resists scale and handles freezes better, and copper is rigid, UV-tolerant and proven but pricier and vulnerable to aggressive water.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper lasts but is pricier and can pinhole in corrosive water.
